How this harbor is built

Drawn natively. Every frame is maths per character.

1. No engine. No WebGL. No 3D models.

One plain JS file draws on a 2D canvas. That is all.

harbor.js  -->  2D canvas  -->  text

2. One ray per character

Each character cell asks: what do I see first, looking through me?

          +---+---+---+
 eye      | . | : | # |
  o ----- | - | = | @ |  1 ray
          | * | + | % |  per cell
          +---+---+---+

 RAY > HIT > LIGHT > GLYPH > COLOUR

3. Shapes are formulas, not meshes

A ray solves each formula to find where it touches. No triangles.

Sea and island are height fields. Sails are curved cloth. Grass and spray are dots.

4. Big things are many small shapes

The ship is 169 shapes. The whole scene is 444. A ray still tests about one.

6. Cost is the cell count

Rays per frame at the default glyph size. Bigger screen, more cells, until the cell cap holds 4K down.

All the detail (long)

Cells per screen

One ray per cell, plus shadow rays for lit cells. Glyphs are 0.9 of the old 6.5 to 11 px range up to 2560 by 1440 CSS pixels, and taller above that, because the cell cap is 76,000 and falls with the square root of the stage area above 2560 by 1440 (about 51,000 cells at 3840 by 2160). Cells are whole device pixels. "Largest glyphs" is the 3 times larger grid a room can fall back to. The counts assume a glyph 0.6 times as wide as tall, a 393 by 852 phone at pixel ratio 3, and desktops at pixel ratio 1. Frame time depends on the device, so it is not listed here.

How the page adapts

  • A frame over 10 ms leaves too little of the 16.7 ms display period for painting. After 20 such frames in a row, outdoors, the page casts every other row each frame (fields).
  • If still slow, the page turns shadow rays off.
  • If still slow, glyphs grow 15% at a time: not at all outdoors on a desktop, up to 1.5 times as big outdoors on a touch device, up to 3 times in a room. Crossing a door resets them to the default size.
  • After 90 fast frames in a row (under 6 ms) the glyphs shrink again, then fields end.
  • Every canvas draws at the display's pixel ratio, and none is bigger than 4096 x 4096 device pixels.
  • Each ray tests only the shapes whose screen box covers its 8-cell tile, nearest first, and stops at the first hit.
  • Measured at 1440 x 900 over five views: the scene has 444 shapes (275 outside, 169 on the ship). For each list a ray walks, it meets 1.3 to 2.6 candidate shapes, box-tests 0.6 to 1.7 of them, and runs the exact formula 0.3 to 1.2 times. The 5 tree crowns hold 43 leaf clumps in all.

The rest

  • Renderer. Each rendered frame casts one ray per character cell from your eye, except cells still covered by intro noise. Buildings, the dock and the hull are convex solids made of planes; the hull leaves its deck open over the hatch to the gun deck. Masts, barrels, broadleaf trunk feet and the lighthouse are true cones with smooth normals. Gun barrels and carriage wheels use cones on horizontal axes. Rocks are ellipsoids. Tree trunks and branches above the broadleaf trunk feet use tapered round limbs along any axis. A broadleaf crown groups its leaf clumps in one bounding box. Smooth 3D noise changes each clump's radius and makes gaps in its surface. One or two noise samples locate the near surface; a gap can cause another sample for the far surface. A ray tests clumps in order of entry into their outer spheres. It stops when no remaining clump can be nearer than its hit. Sails and the flag are curved cloth. Inside the cloth's box, a ray samples the gap between the ray and the cloth. Near-parallel rays subdivide intervals that can contain crossings and select the nearest crossing inside the cloth's outline. Each bracketed crossing uses a halving and straight-line steps. Ship rigging and the masthead pennants use projected lines. Tall grass uses projected tufts, and fountain water uses projected droplets. Palm fronds use projected spines with paired leaflets; distant palms show fewer leaflets or only spines. They check the depth buffer instead of ray intersections. See the harbor README for the fountain's streams and landing surfaces. The sea is a height field of travelling waves, ray marched per cell; the small boats ride it. See the harbor README for the ship's wind-driven sway. Low, dark hills rise from the sea on part of the horizon, just lighter than the sky. The island is a smooth height field of rolling hills from value noise, levelled under the objects that stand on it. The page samples it once on a 25 cm grid, and each ray starts its march where it can first reach the highest ground nearby. The roads are curves through control points. A 50 cm grid stores the centre-line distance and half width for the road whose edge is nearest.
  • Light. Exterior lamps, lit windows, the ship's lanterns and the beacon light nearby surfaces, with brightness that decreases with distance. Exterior solids cast shadows when shadow rays are enabled, except the thin raised cabin trim, the companionway ladder and the ship's wheel. A shadow ray skips the solid being lit, because a convex solid cannot shade its own lit face. Only hatch parts above the deck form the grouped shadow caster, with one bounding box. The hatch boards and ladder receive light without shadow rays. The hatch lantern emits light without shadow rays. Leaves receive lamplight without shadow rays. Crowns cast no lamp shadows because the lamps are below them. Lamplight on leaves is compressed to prevent flat bright patches. See the harbor README for the trees' appearance and motion. The lighthouse beam sweeps; the moon gives a dim fill that is stronger on slopes facing it. Ship sails and the flag bypass lamp and shadow calculations; sails are brightest where they belly out farthest, and fog still dims them. The black flag draws only its outline, its folds and its pale skull and crossbones. The house's lamp and fire, the cabin's hanging lantern and the gun deck lanterns light nearby surfaces without shadow rays. The cabin's wall lantern glows but lights no other surface. The sea shows Fresnel reflection, moon and lamp highlights and foam. Fog and contact shadows add depth.
  • Clouds. Three cloud layers, with bases at 260, 480 and 900 m, drift downwind; the nearer layers move faster as you walk. Each layer is a sheet of fractal value noise, stretched along the wind, with its domain warped by more noise. Cloud fades in above a noise threshold with a soft ramp, so patches thin out to clear sky and dips in the noise leave holes. The ramp widens with distance, and haze hides each sheet beyond 4.5 times its height. A few cumulus clumps with flat bases and lumpy tops drift with the lowest layer, on billboards that face you. Cloud density picks glyphs from .:~-=+* in the dim colour of the empty sky; the star hash of each cell breaks bands of one glyph into grain. Only edges where the cloud thins toward the moon are pale, and those nearest the moon are palest. Thin cloud dims the stars and the moon, and thick cloud hides them. A buffer of 1° sky texels keeps the cloud density and moon-side rim of each direction. Without reduced motion, a texel refreshes about ten times a second when a sky cell reads it. The elevation rows take turns to spread refresh work across frames. Turning reuses fresh texels but computes unseen or expired texels. With reduced motion, each render refreshes the texels it reads, including after movement or a change to the motion setting.
  • Characters. Brightness usually picks a glyph from the density ramp .`',:;~-=+*#%@▒▓█ and one of eight colour levels per material. Ground grass uses mostly short marks and sparse tall blades; shade increases gaps and reduces tall blades. The renderer caps each tuft's projected height at 1/14 of the screen height before rounding blades to whole cells. Blades use | ( ) \ / with pale moonlit tips. Far tufts are dimmer. Leaves pick glyphs from ,.:;&%@ by brightness, one for each 20 cm patch, and draw no outline edges. Bark uses strokes that follow its limb on screen, with ( ) on the sides of upright trunks; palm rings and birch marks cross the limb. Road dirt, muted earth, pale paving marks and foam keep their texture glyphs with a minimum brightness that decreases in fog. Random 2 cm specks vary ground blade lean and blend the greens and the edges between grass, sand and road. The fountain pool and upper bowl use animated texture glyphs instead of the density ramp. Fountain droplets use : ' ,; spray and splashes also use ., and mist uses . `. The house's paintings are glyph textures that harbor/art.py converts ahead of time from public-domain works. Each texel keeps one glyph from .:-=+*#%@ and one of a few colours; room light sets the colour level. A painting far from you reads a half or quarter size copy of its texture. Flames in the house use fixed glyphs from an orange base to pale tips, so the idle house needs no redraws. Windows and gun ports are openings that each room shares with its outside walls. A ray through one goes on in the scene on the other side, from where the room stands in it, so the room shows through its panes and the outside through its openings. Each frame culls the far side only for the screen area of the openings, so no window needs its own rendering pass. An edge glyph (| - / \ _) is drawn wherever one surface meets a farther one, and along hill crests.
  • Frame loop. requestAnimationFrame moves you, the sea, the ship and its sails, and redraws the grid on a canvas. A ray tests solids whose screen box reaches its tile of cells, nearest first. It stops when no remaining solid can be nearer than its hit. After the intro, the canvas keeps unchanged rows. Devices without (pointer: coarse) draw rows with changed cells again from their full text-run positions. Touch devices redraw only each run of changed cells and its neighbouring cells; runs fewer than five cells apart share one redraw. Grass and tree wind animation uses a 12 Hz clock; other scene changes can repaint grass and tree cells between wind updates. Tree bending increases with height along the shared wind direction; leaf clumps also move across that direction. Fountain water moves on a 24 Hz clock; other scene changes can repaint its cells between water updates. The mini map draws again each frame, even when its cells do not change. The pop-up sign also draws again each frame; the cells it covered draw again when it moves or closes. Devices with (pointer: coarse) draw one glyph per fillText call to limit WebKit's text cache. Every canvas draws at the display's pixel ratio, so the browser never rescales it, and each cell is a whole number of device pixels wide and tall so glyphs stay sharp. Other devices draw one run of one colour per call. The backing-store area stays at or below 4096 * 4096 device pixels; this does not limit each dimension to 4096. Large high-DPR windows use a lower rendering ratio, which can fall below 1. Grid measurement clears the complete canvas, including padding, even when the backing-store size does not change. The intro overlay uses CSS dimensions to cover the transformed canvas at any rendering ratio. The first grid measurement waits for fonts; if the requested font fails to load, the scene uses a fallback font. The default glyph height is 0.9 times the old one, as if you had pressed Cmd-minus once, so the same view uses about 1.23 times as many cells. Up to 2560 by 1440 CSS pixels the glyph height is 0.9 of the old default; a larger stage gets taller glyphs because the cell cap falls with the square root of the stage area (about 51000 cells at 3840 by 2160), and each cell is still a whole number of device pixels. On a desktop the outdoor glyph size is fixed by the stage size alone, so walking and turning never change it and the lens never changes. On a touch device it stays fixed unless fields and shadows cannot hold the frame rate; then it may grow up to 1.5 times and shrinks back when frames are fast. Rooms grow their glyphs up to 3 times when they render slowly. Resizes wait until the viewport settles, then update the canvas immediately before the next draw. Slow outdoor rendering first switches to fields: each frame casts and repaints every other row, so each row refreshes 30 times a second, then disables exterior shadow rays, if enabled. Fields stay off with reduced motion and indoors, and end when a full frame would cost under 9 ms for 90 frames. Slow rendering in a room disables shadow rays, if enabled, then uses larger glyphs with fewer cells; fast rendering restores a finer grid. Missed frames also reduce detail relative to the measured display refresh rate. Before counting slower callbacks as painting load, the renderer skips one repaint to measure the display period without that load. This sample also detects display refresh changes during continuous input. Each door or hatch crossing requests the same unloaded sample, so timing from the previous scene does not hide room painting load. The house redraws only after movement, looking, resizing, or another change that requires a new frame. The cabin and gun deck also redraw while swaying with the hull. Reduced motion stops exterior animation and idle ship-room redraws; see the harbor README for ship motion.
  • Intro rendering. The sweep shifts a single cached 8x8 glyph tile over cells that still hide the scene. It uses the same canvas without a cached scene. Each intro frame ends with a black canvas fill at opacity 1 - progress * progress. Desktop drawing keeps the colour runs; touch drawing sends each glyph directly to the existing single-glyph painter. The mini map, signs, and touch pad stay hidden until the intro ends.
  • Controls. WASD or arrows walk and turn, R and F look up and down, the mouse looks around, Enter opens what you point at. On a phone, the bottom-left pad walks; drag on the right to look around. The pad and map controls respect safe-area insets. See the harbor README for sign links and keyboard access.
  • Sound. The first click or tap creates one AudioContext and fetches six mono loops; the speaker button in the bottom-left corner mutes them. Each loop runs through a gain and an HRTF PannerNode with the inverse distance model, so it fades with distance from you. The sea, wind and lantern loops move to their nearest emitter: the waterline or a ship side, a tree, or a gun deck lantern. The listener and the emitters update at most 20 times a second, not every frame, and move smoothly between updates. Rooms lower the outdoor loops and turn on their fire or lantern. A muted or hidden page suspends the context, so sound costs no CPU then.
  • Mini map. Outside, M picks a point on the text map in the corner. Press M again to fill the screen with the map. Use the arrows to choose a point. Enter or a tap selects a destination. See the map travel guidance for routes and reduced motion. See the house, cabin and gun deck guidance for door and hatch crossings and controls inside.
  • Content. harbor/build.py builds the points of interest with the README's Features list and More docs row, and the changelog.
  • Deploy. See the deployment guidance. The page has no runtime dependencies; nothing in harbor/ reaches a Crewship host or worker image.
